# Hold-scoped push-to-talk: what the benchmarks say

## Question

In push-to-talk the key release is an explicit end-of-utterance signal, yet
[`AudioPipelineService.ProcessBatch`](../../src/Parlotype.Platform/Audio/AudioPipelineService.cs)
still cuts the recording whenever trailing silence exceeds `WaitTime`
(0.5–3 s, user-selectable). The proposal: in push-to-talk, suppress that
mid-recording flush and transcribe the whole hold in one decode.

Before committing, two things needed measuring:

1. What does one long decode cost, and where does it stop working — **per engine**?
2. Does the extra context actually improve the output?

## Method

`datasets/long-audio-ladder` (new, generated by `datasets/build-long-audio-ladder.py`)
concatenates LibriSpeech test-other utterances with fixed-length silence gaps into a
duration ladder: 15/30/60/120/300/600 s at 1.2 s gaps, plus 60/300 s at 3.5 s gaps.
Reference text is the concatenation of the source references, which stays exact because
no word is created or destroyed at a seam. Two gap widths matter because the gap is what
trips the flush: 1.2 s trips the 0.5/1 s thresholds, 3.5 s trips all of them.

Each rung was run through four arms via the benchmark's existing
`vad.silenceThresholdMs` pipeline simulation: `500`, `1000`, `3000`, and `null`
(one-shot — the proposed hold-scoped behaviour).

## Finding 1 — Parakeet hard-crashes past 400 s

Feeding sherpa-onnx more than 400 s of audio in one decode throws
`SEHException` out of native ONNX Runtime:

```
Non-zero status code returned while running Add node. Name:'/layers.0/self_attn/Add_2'
Attempting to broadcast an axis by a dimension other than 1. 1886 by 6886
```

Bisected with `--probe` to a sharp cliff: **400 s decodes, 405 s crashes**. The numbers
explain themselves — the failing sample was 550.9 s of extracted speech, and
550.9 s ÷ 80 ms per encoder frame = 6886 frames; 6886 − 1886 = 5000, a 5000-frame
positional-encoding buffer. 5000 × 80 ms = exactly 400.0 s.

This is not a graceful failure. It is a C++ exception crossing a C boundary out of a
native decode, so recovery is not something to rely on.

Today the app never reaches it: the 30 s `MaxBatchBufferSamples` force-flush caps every
decode. **Removing that cap without an engine-aware replacement introduces a hard crash.**

## Finding 2 — Parakeet silently loses content well before it crashes

Long before 400 s, one-shot Parakeet starts dropping text. Word counts against the
reference, one-shot arm:

| rung | ref words | hyp words | kept | WER |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 32 | 32 | 100% | 0.0 |
| 30 s | 90 | 89 | 99% | 2.2 |
| 60 s | 124 | 124 | 100% | 4.0 |
| 120 s | 269 | 161 | **60%** | 43.2 |
| 300 s | 683 | 325 | **48%** | 54.7 |

CER tracks WER almost exactly (54.2 vs 54.7 at 300 s), which is the signature of *missing*
text rather than misrecognised text. The transcript does reach content near the end of the
reference, so this is not a clean truncation at a fixed point — the decoder is skipping
material throughout.

**Parakeet's usable one-shot ceiling is ~60 s, not 400 s.**

## Finding 3 — Whisper is immune

Whisper Base, same ladder, same one-shot arm:

| rung | kept | WER | RTF | RAM Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 103% | 6.1 | 0.009 | 33 MB |
| 60 s | 101% | 11.1 | 0.005 | 9 MB |
| 300 s | 99% | 11.8 | 0.005 | 91 MB |
| 600 s | 99% | 12.6 | 0.004 | 246 MB |

Flat WER, full word retention, linear RAM, no crash. Whisper chunks internally at 30 s,
so length is not a correctness question for it — only a latency and memory one.

## Finding 4 — the win is real, but WER can't see it

WER strips punctuation and casing, so it is blind to the actual complaint. Tested
directly on the one sample with punctuated reference text and real pauses
(`Russian-accent--with-pauses-001`):

```
flush500   Hello? How are you doing today? What your plans for V7. Can we meet tomorrow? Yeah.
flush1000  Hello? How are you doing today? What your plans for V7. Can we meet tomorrow? Yeah.
flush3000  Hello? How are we doing today? What'd your plans for this evening? Can we meet tomorrow? Bye-bye.
one-shot   Hello? How are we doing today? What'd your plans for this evening? Can we meet tomorrow? Bye-bye.
REFERENCE  Hello. How are you doing today? What are your plans for this evening? Can we meet tomorrow? Goodbye.
```

A cut through "this evening" turned it into "V7". The mechanism behind the original report
is confirmed: flushing mid-sentence corrupts words at the boundary and forces punctuation
to be decided on a fragment.

`flush3000` and `one-shot` are byte-identical here, because no pause in this sample exceeds
3 s. That also explains why the reporter, already on Very Long (3 s), still sees the problem:
their remaining bad cuts come from pauses longer than 3 s, which no available setting covers.

## Finding 5 — the existing 30 s force-flush is itself a bug

When the buffer overflows, `ProcessBatch` queues `_sampleBuffer.ToArray()` — the **raw**
buffer, skipping the VAD extraction every other path applies. It shows up in the data:

| rung | flush1000 | flush3000 |
|---|---|---|
| 300 s | 4.8 (100% kept) | 16.2 (89% kept) |
| 600 s | 4.9 (100% kept) | 19.7 (85% kept) |

`flush3000` degrades only because it hits the 30 s cap more often. Whatever ceiling
replaces it should chunk on VAD boundaries, not dump raw audio.

## What this means for the design

The original proposal holds for the realistic case and needs a guard for the rest:

- **Suppress the mid-recording flush in push-to-talk.** Confirmed equal-or-better up to
  ~60 s, and it fixes the boundary corruption that motivated the request.
- **The ceiling is per-engine, and it is a quality ceiling, not just a cost ceiling.**
  Parakeet ~60–90 s; Whisper effectively unbounded (latency is the only limit).
- **Above the ceiling, chunk on VAD boundaries** — never raw-dump, and never let Parakeet
  see more than 400 s in one call.
- Cloud engines were not benchmarked (bring-your-own-key, billable). Note that neither
  `OpenAiCompatibleSpeechRecognizer` nor `XaiGrokSpeechRecognizer` has a client-side size
  guard, and 16 kHz mono 16-bit WAV runs 32 KB/s — OpenAI's 25 MB request limit lands at
  about 13 minutes.

## Harness changes made along the way

- `PipelineSimulator` — the 30 s ceiling was a hardcoded const; now a swept parameter.
- `VadConfig.MaxBufferSeconds`, and `vad.maxBufferSeconds` as a sweep axis.
- `vad.silenceThresholdMs: null` is now expressible in a sweep (selects one-shot).
- `SweepConfig.Parakeet` / `.LlamaCpp` — sweeps silently dropped the engine block, so every
  sweep was Whisper-only.
- **Bug fix:** the Whisper branch of `Program.cs` resolved `ISpeechRecognizer`, which routes
  through `DelegatingSpeechRecognizer` and reads `SpeechEngine` from the *user's*
  `settings.json`. Since Parakeet became the app default ([ADR-041](../../docs/decisions/041-parakeet-v3-sherpa-onnx.md)),
  every Whisper benchmark config had been silently benchmarking Parakeet. Whisper configs
  now pin the engine and resolve `WhisperSpeechRecognizer` directly. **Whisper results
  produced between ADR-041 and this fix should be treated as suspect.**

## Reproducing

```bash
python datasets/build-long-audio-ladder.py
dotnet run --project src/Parlotype.Benchmark -- sweep \
  --config datasets/long-audio-parakeet-sweep-config.json --datasets datasets --output results
```

The 600 s rung crashes the one-shot arm by design; `datasets/long-audio-oneshot-config.json`
excludes it. To re-pin the ceiling:

```bash
python datasets/build-long-audio-ladder.py --probe 380 440 5
```

then run `datasets/long-audio-probe-config.json` one sample at a time.
